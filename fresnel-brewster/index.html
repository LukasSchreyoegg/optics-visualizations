How it works

The idea. Light is a transverse wave. At an interface its electric field drives the electrons of the material, and the reflected and the refracted wave are the sum of the waves sent out by all these small dipoles. What happens depends on the direction in which E oscillates: perpendicular to the plane of incidence (TE) or in it (TM). Matching the fields at the interface (tangential E and H continuous) gives Snell's law and the Fresnel equations.

n1 sin θi = n2 sin θt,   θr = θi
rTE = (n1 cos θi − n2 cos θt) / (n1 cos θi + n2 cos θt),   rTM = (n2 cos θi − n1 cos θt) / (n2 cos θi + n1 cos θt)
tTE = 2n1 cos θi / (n1 cos θi + n2 cos θt),   tTM = 2n1 cos θi / (n2 cos θi + n1 cos θt)
R = |r|2,   T = (n2 cos θt)/(n1 cos θi) · |t|2

The factor in T accounts for the different speed and the different width of the beam in the second medium. Sign conventions for rTM differ between textbooks; the reflected powers R do not depend on them. For unpolarised light the powers of TE and TM are averaged.

The picture. All rays have the same width; their brightness is their power, drawn with the usual display gamma, so a ray with 4 % of the power really looks like 4 % of the light.

Brewster angle. For TM light the dipoles in the second medium oscillate perpendicular to the refracted ray, in the plane of incidence. A dipole emits nothing along its axis (radiated power ∝ sin2ϑ, as on the Rayleigh page). If the reflected ray would run exactly along that axis, there is no TM reflection. This happens when reflected and refracted ray are perpendicular, θi + θt = 90°, which with Snell's law gives tan θB = n2/n1. Reflection at this angle is a simple polariser.

Total internal reflection. Coming from the denser medium, sin θt would exceed 1 above sin θc = n2/n1. Then cos θt becomes imaginary, |r| = 1 and all light is reflected. Behind the interface only an evanescent wave remains, which decays with the depth

d = λ / (2π √(n12 sin2θi − n22))   (here for λ = 550 nm).

Not included. Absorption (all refractive indices are real), dispersion (fixed values near 589 nm: water 1.333, glass 1.50, diamond 2.417), the phase jump on reflection, multiple reflections in a plate.
